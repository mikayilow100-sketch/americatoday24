Houston limits police cooperation with ICE after Democrats sweep Texas races

By AmericaToday24 News Desk · Nov 6, 2026

HOUSTON — Houston Mayor John Whitmire has issued an executive order limiting how city police work with federal immigration agents during traffic stops, days after Democrats won statewide offices in Texas.

Under the order, officers may contact Immigration and Customs Enforcement only if a person has committed a jailable offense and has an ICE warrant. In nonviolent cases, police will cite and release people instead of holding them for ICE. Police Chief J. Noe Diaz said officers will not contact ICE over an administrative warrant if the person is not being arrested. Whitmire said the aim is to reduce fear in Houston’s communities, adding that “elections matter.”

In April, Gov. Greg Abbott threatened to withhold about $110 million in state public safety grants after the City Council dropped a rule requiring officers to wait for ICE at traffic stops, arguing it violated a 2017 state law that bars limits on cooperation with federal immigration authorities. The council later revised the policy. Abbott lost his re-election bid this month to Democrat Gina Hinojosa, who has criticized his immigration policies.

Abbott’s office did not immediately respond to a request for comment. Houston Controller Chris Hollins called the order “a promising first step,” and the mayors of Austin and San Antonio said they would review their own cities’ policies.
